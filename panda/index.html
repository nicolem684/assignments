Panda Diplomacy

How the spatial distribution of pandas in captivity showcases China's diplomatic relationships.

Nicole Manning Lorino

Giant Pandas are part of the bear family and are endemic to modern day China. Their natural habitat has shrunk, limiting them to the temperate forest mountain regions in the Sichuan province near Chengdu. Pandas were long classified as an endangered species, but recently switched to being a vulnerable species with a global population of roughly a little under 2,000. This switch comes as a result of long term conservation efforts from China and international partners. (Swaisgood).

Governments of China have long used pandas for diplomacy reasons and have gifted other nations pandas as far back as the Tang Dynasty in the 7th century, when Empress Wu Zeitan supposedly sent pandas to Japan (Holland). The People’s Republic of China has been giving other countries pandas since its formation in the 1940’s, sending their first “panda ambassadors” to the USSR in 1957 (Taylor).

In 1985, China switched their policy to loaning out pandas instead of gifting them, resulting in them retaining legal ownership and the right to revoke pandas from host countries. Countries with pandas gifted to them prior to the policy change retained ownership, but eventually those pandas and their offspring passed away. Mexico is the only country besides China that currently owns a panda. Their panda, Xin Xin, is the granddaughter of Mexico’s original panda pair given to them in 1975.

The modern panda diplomacy program has evolved into a conservation program, with host zoos establishing breeding programs and spreading awareness about the vulnerable species. The program is still largely used as a way for China to deploy soft power and strengthen relationships they wish to develop, but the program has had other successful outcomes.

China offers loans to countries they wish to establish closer ties and symbolize their friendship with. These loans come with a hefty price, with an average annual fee of $1 million USD for zoos in the US (Ding). This payment is mostly meant to support conservation efforts. This number only represents the amount paid to China every year, and does not include money spent on habitat, operations, or program support. Receiving a panda loan thus comes down to a country’s relationship with China and the ability to fund the endeavor.
